## Verified in this development environment

- The Python package compiles without syntax errors.
- The automated suite passes on the available Python runtime.
- Synthetic camera startup automatically creates a perspective bed scene and a known multi-point mapping.
- Lens-calibration capture/solve code, bed homography, rectification, workpiece detection, SVG parsing, placement, path ordering, and G-code generation are covered by unit or integration tests.
- The local HTTP application starts and reports healthy status.
- The POSIX serial transport is exercised through a Linux pseudoterminal, including line framing, raw GRBL status queries, controller acknowledgements, photography-position parking, arming, and a complete streamed job.
- The complete simulated workflow has been exercised through the HTTP API:
  - park at the configured camera pose;
  - analyze the sample SVG;
  - generate a dry frame containing no laser-enable command;
  - execute that dry frame without arming;
  - reject a positive-power job while unarmed;
  - arm temporarily and complete the same job against the simulator;
  - disarm automatically after completion.
- Compact commands such as `G0X10Y10` and `M4S10` are recognized by the safety parser.
- The browser diagnostic console accepts read-only identity/status queries plus `M5`; direct manual motion and `M3`/`M4` commands are rejected even while armed.
- Coordinate offsets and unmodelled modal commands such as `G92` are rejected from streamed jobs.
- Out-of-bounds coordinates, rapid travel while laser state is active, unsupported arcs/modal changes, and jobs that do not end in `M5` are rejected.
- Disarming during an active powered job requests a stop, revokes the job's laser authorization, sends `M5`, and prevents a later path from re-enabling output.

## Not yet physically verified

The following values cannot be responsibly guessed and remain locked or conservative until measured on the actual machine:

- controller protocol and firmware identity;
- serial device path and baud rate;
- controller laser-mode configuration and usable `S` range;
- whether `M3`, `M4`, or another controller-specific mode is appropriate;
- machine origin, axis directions, homing behavior, and reachable laser-head area;
- laser-head offset relative to the original nozzle/tool reference;
- repeatable bed photography pose;
- C920 focus/exposure values for the installed lighting and mounting height;
- lens-calibration residuals from real checkerboard photographs;
- bed-mapping residuals and repeatability across cold starts;
- parallax error at different workpiece thicknesses;
- real dry-motion and low-power framing behavior.

## Hardware bring-up gate

Do not enable `machine.allow_motion` or use `./run-hardware.sh` until the camera and controller probes have been captured and reviewed. Keep the laser power lead physically disconnected during early motion/controller identification where practical. The first physical execution should be a small, centrally located dry frame generated by the application, followed by repeatability measurements before any powered laser test.
